CarnivorousLog privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026. Effective 23 November 2026. Google Play package: com.hezarfen.carnivorouslog. Samsung Galaxy Store package: com.hezarfen.carnivorouslog.galaxy. iOS App Store bundle identifier: com.hezarfen.carnivorouslog.

In short

CarnivorousLog is a care log for carnivorous plants: your pots, what you grow in them, care readings and reminders. You can use it without an account. Your records are stored on your device. The free version shows ads from Google AdMob; Premium removes them. We do not sell data.

Who we are

CarnivorousLog is published by ONOUR IMPRAM VENTURES LTD, a company registered in the United Kingdom under company number 17429906, which is responsible for the personal data described here. Contact: onour@onourimpram.com.

Data that stays on your device

The following is stored only in the app’s storage on your device and is not sent to us:

  • Your pots: name, size, type and notes
  • What you keep in each pot: name, species, count, type, the date added and a note
  • Care readings: TDS level, soil moisture, trap count, dormancy, temperature and pest level, with the date and a note
  • The reminders you set, and any custom trackers you create, with their entries (date, value and note)
  • In the Samsung Galaxy Store build, your answer to the app’s advertising consent prompt. This stays on your device and is not sent to us.
  • Language, theme and notification settings

Service providers

  • Google AdMob shows ads in the free version. The data it processes is described under Advertising below.
  • RevenueCat checks whether Premium is active, lists the available plans, and handles requests to buy a plan or restore purchases. The app does not give RevenueCat a user ID, account name or email address. Your plant records are never sent to RevenueCat.
  • In the Google Play build, Google Play processes the purchase itself under your Google account. The Samsung Galaxy Store build uses Samsung In-App Purchase. The iOS build uses Apple App Store in-app purchase. Each build connects to its store through RevenueCat.
  • Sentry receives crash reports and performance traces from release builds, on Android and iOS. Performance tracing is sampled at 20% of transactions. When the app catches an error in its error boundary, it sends the error and the component stack. For error reports raised in the app’s JavaScript code, the app removes any email address, IP address and username from the report’s user data, along with request cookies, Authorization and Cookie headers, and the device name, before the report leaves the device. Sentry’s documentation states that this filtering does not apply to native events from Android or iOS. The app does not set a user identifier in Sentry, and it does not filter or limit the breadcrumbs (a record of recent app events) that Sentry’s SDK can attach to a report.
  • Restore Purchases, in Settings or on the purchase screen, asks RevenueCat to restore purchases from the store that build uses. If you have Premium, Manage Subscription opens Google Play’s subscriptions page in the Google Play build and Apple’s subscriptions page on iOS, and in the Galaxy Store build it asks you to open the store app you subscribed with; otherwise it opens the purchase screen.
  • Links you open are handed to your browser and that site's own privacy practices apply.

RevenueCat privacy policy: https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy. Google privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy.

Advertising

The free version of CarnivorousLog shows ads from Google AdMob.

Premium removes the ads.

Google’s documentation for the Google Mobile Ads SDK states that on Android the SDK automatically collects and shares, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention, your device’s IP address, which may be used to estimate its general location; product interactions such as app launches, taps and video views; diagnostic information such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage; and device identifiers such as the Android advertising ID and app set ID. For iOS, Google lists crash logs, performance data, a device ID such as the advertising identifier, advertising data such as the ads you have seen, coarse location including location derived from your IP address, and product interactions. Google uses this data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention under its own privacy policy. On iOS, the app’s own code does not read the advertising identifier; it only checks and requests tracking permission, as described below.

In every build, Google’s consent flow (User Messaging Platform) runs before the Google Mobile Ads SDK starts, and ads start only when that flow says they may be requested. If the flow fails, the app uses a consent state saved on an earlier launch; otherwise ads stay off. Where Google’s consent platform requires it, Settings shows Ad privacy choices, which reopens Google’s consent form. In the Google Play and iOS builds, the app adds no personalisation setting to ad requests, so the Google Mobile Ads SDK follows the consent recorded by Google’s form; outside the regions where that form applies, ads may be personalised. If you do not agree to personalised ads, Google can still show non-personalised ads, which use the same technical data for delivery, frequency limits, reporting and fraud prevention but are not based on your past activity. On Android, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings.

Your pots, plants, care readings, notes and reminders are never sent to AdMob or to any advertiser.

In the Samsung Galaxy Store build, ad requests are non-personalised in every region until you give explicit consent. If Google’s consent platform reports that its form applies, ads are personalised only if you chose personalised ads in that form. Otherwise, they are personalised only after you answer yes in the app’s own prompt or turn on the Personalized ads switch in Settings. Answering no, not answering, or an answer the app cannot read keeps requests non-personalised. If reading Google’s consent status fails or takes longer than five seconds, the app treats it as no consent.

The Galaxy Store build’s own prompt appears only after ads have started. It offers two equally styled buttons, to allow or not allow personalised ads, with neither pre-selected, and a link to this policy. If you dismiss it with Back, it returns at the next launch and requests stay non-personalised until then. You can change your answer at any time with the Personalized ads switch in Settings, which is shown where this prompt applies and not to Premium users.

On iOS, the app asks for App Tracking Transparency permission after Google’s consent flow allows ad requests and before the Google Mobile Ads SDK starts. It asks only while you have not yet answered, and it does not ask again after you answer. It does not ask if ads cannot be requested, or if Premium is already known to be active when ads start up. Android builds never show this request.

The iOS tracking request says tracking is used only to show ads that better match your interests, and that your plant profiles and care records are never used for advertising. You can allow or decline. If you decline, Apple’s documentation states that the advertising identifier returns all zeros instead of your device’s identifier. Declining, or a request that fails, does not stop ads: when Google’s consent flow allows ads, the banner is shown whatever you answer, unless you have Premium. You can change your tracking choice later in iOS Settings.

How Google uses data from partner apps: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

What we do not do

  • Your records are not uploaded, and we run no server that stores your personal data.
  • No account is required.
  • The app does not collect precise location, your contacts, call or SMS history, health or fitness data, biometric data or microphone audio.
  • There is no usage analytics service in the app. Besides the advertising data described above, release builds send Sentry crash reports and performance traces. Performance tracing is sampled at 20% of transactions.
  • No data sale, and no sharing with advertising data brokers.

Permissions

The permission names in brackets below are Android manifest permissions. On iOS, the app asks for tracking permission and for permission to show reminder notifications, as described below.

  • Internet and network state (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE), used for ads, Premium purchases and crash reports.
  • Advertising ID (AD_ID) and ad services (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS), declared by Google's Mobile Ads SDK and used by Google AdMob as described above. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android settings.
  • Purchases: in the Google Play build, Google Play billing (BILLING), used when you buy or restore Premium. In the Samsung Galaxy Store build, Samsung In-App Purchase, through Samsung’s in-app purchase library, which only that build includes. The Galaxy Store build declares Samsung’s billing permission (com.samsung.android.iap.permission.BILLING) and also Google Play billing (com.android.vending.BILLING). On iOS, Apple App Store in-app purchase. Each build reaches its store through RevenueCat.
  • App Tracking Transparency, the iOS tracking permission, which the app requests before it starts Google’s ads; the request text says tracking is used only to show ads that better match your interests. Google’s documentation states that the advertising identifier may be used for third-party advertising and analytics. The app’s own code does not read the IDFA. Declining does not stop ads when Google’s consent flow allows them. The iOS build declares no other permission usage string: no camera, photo library, microphone, location or Face ID.
  • Android notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS), for the reminders you set in the app. Android asks you before the app can show a notification, and you can turn notifications off for the app in Android settings. On iOS, the reminder flow uses iOS’s own notification permission prompt when permission has not been granted.
  • Vibration (VIBRATE), declared by the haptics and notifications libraries the app includes.
  • Wake lock (WAKE_LOCK) and foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE), declared by libraries the app includes. Neither gives the app access to your personal data.
  • Launcher badge permissions (READ_APP_BADGE and the badge permissions of several device makers' launchers), declared by the notifications library for app icon badges. They give the app no access to your data.
  • Other manifest permissions (the Google install referrer service and the cloud messaging receive permission) are declared by libraries the app includes. The released build contains no Firebase project configuration.

Your choices and deletion

Your records are stored on your device. Uninstalling the app deletes them, unless you used your phone's own backup feature, which is outside our control. There is no in-app setting to turn crash reporting off. Purchase records stay with the store you bought through (Google Play, Samsung or Apple) and with RevenueCat, and crash reports stay with Sentry; to ask about those records, email us with the purchase's transaction ID or the time of the crash and your phone model. We respond within thirty days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or object to the processing of your personal data. Send requests by email; we will not ask for more information than we need to respond. In the United Kingdom you can also complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

Children

CarnivorousLog is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal data from them.

Changes

We update this policy before any change to the app’s data practices is released. The effective date is shown at the top of this page.
