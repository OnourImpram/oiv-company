We look beyond what technology can do. We ask how people come to trust it, what it remembers, and what supports its claims. This connects our research, software and product work.

01

Human insight

Start with how people use technology, not just what the technology can do. Behaviour, context and uncertainty shape the questions we ask.

02

Visible evidence

Examine the records behind a claim. Distinguish an observed result from an assumption and make the limits of the evidence clear.

03

User control

Keep attention on how people inspect, correct and direct a system. Control should be part of the design, not an afterthought.

Work with OIV

A clear question.
A defined piece of work.

For product teams and organisations working with AI. Start with the decision you need to make, then agree the scope, access and deliverables.

Focused evaluation

Understand the behaviour behind the output.

A scoped evaluation of an AI assistant or agent workflow. Depending on the agreed scope, examine behavioural risks, evidence behind task claims, or memory and user control.

Discuss an evaluation ↗

  1. Define

    Agree the product flows, risk criteria, languages and access needed.

  2. Examine

    Record test conditions and reproducible examples. Separate findings from uncertainties.

  3. Retest

    Prioritise improvements and check the agreed fixes in a follow-up assessment.

Timing and deliverables are agreed after scoping. Risky model behaviour alone does not establish clinical harm. An evaluation is not a safety certification.

Research and founder

Two disciplines.
One way of working.

OIV was founded by Onour Impram, a clinical psychologist and AI researcher. The company connects questions about human behaviour and trust with the practical work of building and examining software.

Research informs the questions. Engineering makes them concrete. Findings should remain open to inspection and proportionate to the evidence.
